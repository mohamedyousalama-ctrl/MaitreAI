# هريس (Harees)

## Region
Loved across the **Hijaz** and **Eastern Province** (and the wider Gulf); a Ramadan staple.

## Ingredients
**Wheat** and **meat** (usually chicken or lamb) cooked long and beaten to a smooth, uniform
paste; seasoned simply and often finished with **ghee (سمن)**, sometimes dusted with cinnamon
and sugar in sweeter regional takes.

## Taste
Silky, smooth, savoury and rich; almost creamy despite having no cream. Gentle, filling,
easy to eat — a classic iftar comfort.

## Cultural significance
Strongly tied to **Ramadan** and Eid; making harees (hours of beating the wheat) is a communal
labour of love, sometimes done in big batches for the neighbourhood or mosque.

## Rituals
Served hot at iftar, a drizzle of ghee on top; scooped and shared. In some homes a sweet
cinnamon-sugar version appears too.

## Etiquette
Ramadan generosity — extra is cooked to give away. Accept a bowl from a neighbour graciously.

## Recommendation voice
- «الهريس طري وناعم ومشبع — أكلة رمضان أصيلة، تريّح المعدة بعد الصيام.» — **only if on the menu.**
- Distinguish from jareesh honestly if both exist.

## Anecdote
The old test of harees: it should be so smooth "you can't tell the wheat from the meat."

## Regional variations
- **Hijaz/Eastern:** the savoury ghee-topped classic.
- Sweet cinnamon-sugar versions appear in some homes and regions.

## Pairings
Dates, gahwa, Arabic tea at iftar. *(Offer only what the tenant sells.)*

## Modern context
A Ramadan-season hero on many Saudi menus; sold hot, especially around iftar. Wheat-based
(and sometimes ghee-finished) — relevant for gluten/dairy allergies.
