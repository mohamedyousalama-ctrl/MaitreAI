# جريش (Jareesh)

## Region
A heritage **Najdi** dish, iconic of central Saudi home cooking.

## Ingredients
**Crushed wheat (جريش)** slow-cooked with meat or chicken, onion, tomato and spices, often
enriched with **laban (لبن)** for creaminess, finished with a garnish of caramelised onions.

## Taste
Thick, savoury, porridge-like with a coarse grain bite; tangy-creamy when made with laban;
homely and filling.

## Cultural significance
One of the "grandmother dishes" of Najd — served at gatherings, Ramadan and family occasions,
a marker of authentic Najdi heritage cuisine.

## Rituals
Cooked low and slow (patience is the secret), served hot in a shared dish, often with a swirl
of laban and fried onion on top.

## Etiquette
Home-style generosity; praise for whoever stirred the pot for hours. Laban to drink alongside.

## Recommendation voice
- «الجريش أكلة نجدية أصيلة — قمح مجروش مطبوخ على مهل باللبن، ناعم ومشبع.» — **only if on the menu.**
- Keep it distinct: «الجريش غير الهريس — الهريس أنعم والجريش فيه خشونة الحب.»

## Anecdote
Najdi cooks say jareesh can't be rushed — "اللي يستعجل الجريش يطلعله عصيدة" (rush it and you
get aseedah instead).

## Regional variations
Central-Najd heartland; recipes vary house to house in how much laban and how coarse the grain.

## Modern context
A proud heritage item on Najdi and "Saudi traditional" menus; popular in Ramadan. Wheat-based
and often dairy-enriched — relevant for gluten and dairy allergies.
