# مثلوثة (Mathloutha)

## Region
A **Najdi** specialty especially associated with **Al-Qassim (القصيم)** — Buraidah/Unaizah country.

## Ingredients
The name means "the tripled" — a bowl that **combines three heritage dishes**: **jareesh**
(crushed wheat), **margoog** (dough discs) and **qursan** (thin bread), layered with meat,
vegetables and broth, often with laban.

## Taste
Textural and hearty — coarse jareesh grain, chewy margoog dough and soft qursan bread all in
one savoury, comforting bowl. The ultimate Najdi comfort combination.

## Cultural significance
A dish of abundance and heritage pride in Qassim — serving mathloutha says "I gave you
everything at once." A winter and gathering favourite that showcases Najdi grain cooking.

## Rituals
Assembled from the three component dishes into one shared platter; eaten hot, communally.

## Etiquette
Home/gathering generosity; a labour-intensive dish, so serving it honours the guest.

## Recommendation voice
- «المثلوثة تجمع لك الجريش والمرقوق والقرصان في صحن واحد — أكلة قصيمية دسمة ومشبعة.» — **only if on the menu.**
- Be honest it's a combination dish, not a single grain.

## Anecdote
Qassimis joke that mathloutha is "three grandmothers' recipes in one bowl" — and no two make
it the same way.

## Regional variations
Centred on Al-Qassim; component ratios vary by household. Rare outside Najd.

## Pairings
Laban; gahwa and dates after. *(Offer only what the tenant sells.)*

## Modern context
A heritage/comfort showpiece on Najdi and Qassimi menus, especially in winter and Ramadan.
Wheat-heavy and often dairy — relevant for gluten/dairy allergies.
