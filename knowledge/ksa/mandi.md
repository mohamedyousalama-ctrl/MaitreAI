# مندي (Mandi)

## Region
Yemeni in origin, deeply adopted in the **Hijaz** and **Asir**, and popular Kingdom-wide.
The signature of many grill/rice houses in Jeddah, Mecca and the south.

## Ingredients
Basmati rice and meat (usually **lamb or chicken**) traditionally cooked in a **tandoor-style
underground/clay oven (تنور)**, the meat suspended so its dripping fat flavours the rice below;
seasoned with a mandi spice blend and often a hint of smoke.

## Taste
Smoky, tender, aromatic; the meat falls off the bone, the rice is light and infused with
drippings. Milder and smokier than kabsa's tangy loomi profile.

## Cultural significance
The centrepiece of shared feasts and celebrations in the west and south; ordering a whole
mandi for a group is an act of hospitality. A go-to for gatherings and large delivery orders.

## Rituals
Served on a large communal platter, eaten with the right hand from a shared dish; the tender
meat is pulled apart and shared, best cuts pushed toward guests.

## Etiquette
Communal-platter etiquette as with kabsa. Sahawiq (شطة/سحاوق) is passed for those who want heat.

## Recommendation voice
- «المندي عندنا لحم ولا دجاج؟ اللحم مدخّن وطري يفصفص.» — **only the proteins the item lists.**
- «للعائلة، ذبيحة/نص ذبيحة تكفيكم» — **only if the tenant offers those sizes.**

## Anecdote
The tell of good mandi is the smell before the taste — regulars claim they can name the grill
house by the smoke on the rice.

## Regional variations
- **Hijaz/Asir:** the heartland of Saudi mandi.
- Close cousins on the same menus: **مدفون** (buried/roasted), **مضغوط** (pressure-style),
  and Hijazi **رز بخاري** — related but distinct; only treat them as separate items if the
  tenant lists them separately.

## Pairings
Saudi salad, sahawiq, laban; gahwa and dates to finish. *(Offer only what the tenant sells.)*

## Modern context
A delivery favourite for groups; sold by protein and by portion (فردي / نص / ذبيحة كاملة).
Strong basket-builder: mandi + salad + a cold laban + a sweet.
