# مرقوق (Margoog)

## Region
A cornerstone of **Najdi** home cooking, especially in winter and Ramadan.

## Ingredients
Thin discs of **wheat dough (رقاق)** simmered with meat and a medley of vegetables — pumpkin,
zucchini, tomato, eggplant, potato, carrot — in a spiced tomato broth (loomi, black pepper,
turmeric).

## Taste
Hearty, savoury, softly chewy dough in a thick vegetable-and-meat stew; deeply warming.
Comfort food, not a showpiece.

## Cultural significance
The dish of cold nights and Ramadan tables — humble, nourishing, associated with family and
grandmother's cooking. Part of a Najdi family of dough dishes.

## Rituals
Cooked in one big pot and ladled into a shared dish; eaten hot, often to break a fast.

## Etiquette
A home-style dish; served generously, seconds expected. Laban alongside is common.

## Recommendation voice
- «المرقوق أكلة شتوية دافية — عجين رقاق مع خضار ولحم في مرقة، تشبع وتدفّي.» — **only if on the menu.**
- Distinguish honestly from its cousins: «المرقوق بالرقاق، والجريش بحب القمح المجروش» — don't merge them.

## Anecdote
Najdis say margoog tastes better the colder it is outside — a rainy Riyadh evening is "margoog weather."

## Regional variations
Najdi heartland. Close cousins on the same menus: **قرصان (qursan)**, **مطازيز (matazeez)** —
all dough-based stews with regional tweaks. Treat as separate only if the tenant lists them.

## Pairings
Laban; gahwa and dates after. *(Offer only what the tenant sells.)*

## Modern context
A heritage/comfort item on Najdi menus, popular in winter and Ramadan. Wheat-dough based, so
relevant to flag for gluten allergies.
