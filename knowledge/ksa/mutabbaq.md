# مطبق (Mutabbaq)

## Region
An iconic **Hijazi** street food — Jeddah and Mecca especially — meaning "folded."

## Ingredients
A very thin stretched **wheat dough** folded around a filling and griddle-fried. Classic
savoury filling: **minced meat, onion, leek and egg**; a popular sweet version is filled with
**banana** (and sometimes cheese), finished with syrup or honey.

## Taste
Crispy-chewy golden pastry; savoury version is spiced and eggy; sweet banana version is soft
and sweet. Handheld, satisfying, a little greasy in the best way.

## Cultural significance
A beloved **Ramadan evening** and street-food classic in the Hijaz; buying mutabbaq hot off
the griddle at night is a nostalgic ritual for many Jeddawis.

## Rituals
Made to order on a hot flat griddle, folded, cut into squares, eaten hot from paper. A
walking-around, after-taraweeh food in Ramadan.

## Etiquette
Casual street food; shared by the square. Tea or laban to wash it down.

## Recommendation voice

- «هش من بره وطري من جوه، ينفع سناك أو مع الشاي.» — describe the real item.

## Anecdote
In Ramadan Jeddah, the mutabbaq stall queues are their own social scene — you catch up with
the neighbourhood while you wait for your square.

## Regional variations
Hijazi heartland; savoury vs sweet (banana) are the two main forms. Cheese and other fillings
appear at some shops.

## Pairings
Arabic tea, laban, Ramadan drinks. *(Offer only what the tenant sells.)*

## Modern context
A street-food and Ramadan staple, common on Hijazi and snack menus and delivery. Contains
wheat and (savoury) egg — relevant for gluten/egg allergies.
