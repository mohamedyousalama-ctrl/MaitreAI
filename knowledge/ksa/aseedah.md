# عصيدة (Aseedah)

## Region
Found across the Kingdom — Najd, Hijaz and especially **Asir/south**, with sweet honey
versions particularly loved in the southwest.

## Ingredients
**Wheat (or sometimes other) flour** cooked with water into a thick, smooth dough, served
with a well of **honey**, **date syrup (دبس)** and **ghee (سمن)** poured on top — or in
savoury versions with broth.

## Taste
Soft, thick, stretchy dough; the sweet version is comforting and rich with honey and ghee;
the savoury version is mild and hearty.

## Cultural significance
A heritage comfort and celebration food; the honey-and-ghee sweet aseedah of Asir is famous
and served at births, Eid and gatherings. Associated with warmth and care.

## Rituals
Served in a wide dish with a central pool of honey/ghee; eaten by pinching pieces of dough and
dipping. Sharing from one dish is the norm.

## Etiquette
Generosity dish — the more honey and ghee, the more honoured the guest.

## Recommendation voice
- «العصيدة تبيها حلوة بالعسل والسمن، ولا عادية؟» — **only the versions the tenant sells.**
- «حلا دافي ومشبع، أصيل من الجنوب.» — describe the real item.

## Anecdote
In Asir, a new mother is fed honey aseedah for strength — it's care you can eat.

## Regional variations
- **Asir/south:** sweet honey-and-ghee aseedah is the celebrated form.
- **Najd/Hijaz:** both sweet and savoury versions exist.

## Pairings
Gahwa and dates; the honey/ghee are part of the dish. *(Offer only what the tenant sells.)*

## Modern context
A heritage dessert/comfort item on traditional Saudi menus; popular in winter, Eid and
Ramadan. Wheat + ghee — relevant for gluten/dairy allergies.
