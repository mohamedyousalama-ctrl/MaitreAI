# لبن (Laban)

## Region
Kingdom-wide everyday drink, deeply tied to Najdi and Gulf meals.

## Ingredients
Cultured, slightly sour **buttermilk/yogurt drink** (لبن رايب), sometimes seasoned (متبّل) with
salt, mint or cumin; served cold. (Note: in Saudi usage «لبن» = the drinkable cultured buttermilk;
«حليب» = plain milk — don't confuse them.)

## Taste
Cool, tangy, lightly salty, refreshing; cuts the richness of spiced rice and grilled meat.
Savoury, not sweet.

## Cultural significance
The default companion to **kabsa, mandi, jareesh and margoog** — a Saudi meal of rice and meat
feels incomplete without a cold laban. Humble, everyday, essential.

## Rituals
Poured cold from the carton or a jug at lunch; passed around the shared platter; drunk
throughout the meal to refresh the palate.

## Etiquette
Offered to everyone at the table; topping up guests' cups is basic hospitality.

## Recommendation voice
- «تحب لبن بارد مع الكبسة؟ يفتح النفس ويخفف الدسم 🥛» — **only if laban is on the menu.**
- Keep it savoury: don't pitch laban as a dessert drink.

## Anecdote
Ask a Najdi what makes kabsa perfect and half the answer is "لبن بارد على جنب" — a cold laban
on the side.

## Regional variations
Ubiquitous; seasoned (متبّل with mint/cumin) versions vary by taste and region.

## Pairings
Kabsa, mandi, jareesh, margoog — the savoury-meal companion. *(Offer only what the tenant sells.)*

## Modern context
Sold chilled by the bottle/cup on nearly every Saudi food menu as the natural meal drink; a
simple, reliable upsell with any rice-and-meat dish. Being pure dairy, it's directly relevant
to dairy-allergy handling.
