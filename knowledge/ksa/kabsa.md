# كبسة (Kabsa)

## Region
The de-facto **national dish** of Saudi Arabia, centred in Najd but eaten Kingdom-wide.

## Ingredients
Long-grain basmati rice cooked in a spiced meat/chicken stock with **بهار الكبسة** (black
lime/loomi, cardamom, cinnamon, cloves, black pepper, saffron, dried lime), tomato,
onion and garlic, topped with the protein — **chicken, lamb, camel, or shrimp (روبيان)** —
and often garnished with fried onions, almonds, pine nuts and raisins.

## Taste
Fragrant, savoury, gently tangy from **loomi (dried black lime)**, rich but not heavy;
the rice carries deep stock flavour. Saffron gives colour and aroma.

## Cultural significance
The dish of gathering and generosity — served on a big communal platter for family lunches,
weddings and guests. A Saudi host proves karam with a mountain of kabsa. It's the default
answer to «وش نطبخ للعزيمة؟».

## Rituals
Traditionally eaten from a **shared platter (طبق/صحن)** on the floor of the majlis, with the
**right hand**, everyone around it. Lunch (الغداء) is the main kabsa meal.

## Etiquette
Eat from the part of the platter in front of you; the host pushes the best cuts of meat
toward the guest. Praise the cook. Laban or Vimto often accompany it.

## Anecdote
Ask ten Saudi families for "the real kabsa" and you'll get ten recipes — everyone's mother
makes the definitive one, and saying otherwise starts a friendly argument.

## Regional variations
- **Najd:** the classic spiced-stock kabsa, saffron and loomi forward.
- **Eastern Province:** overlaps with Gulf **machboos**; shrimp (روبيان) kabsa is loved on the coast.
- **Hijaz:** kabsa is eaten but shares the table with **مندي** and **رز بخاري**.
- **Asir:** local grains and hotter, herbier profiles appear alongside.

## Pairings
Laban or **دقوس** (a tomato-chilli sauce) alongside; gahwa and dates to close; salad.
*(Offer only what the tenant sells.)*

## Modern context
The top-selling item on most Saudi restaurant and delivery menus; sold by protein and by
portion (فردي / عائلي). A reliable upsell anchor: a family kabsa + laban + a sweet.
