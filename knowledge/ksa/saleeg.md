# سليق (Saleeg)

## Region
A **Hijazi** specialty, famously associated with **Taif (الطائف)** and the western highlands.

## Ingredients
Short/medium rice slow-cooked in **milk and chicken broth** until soft and porridge-like,
finished with **ghee (سمن)** and topped with a piece of chicken; served with a dab of
**shatta (شطة)** or dukkah for contrast.

## Taste
Creamy, mild, soft, comforting — almost a savoury rice pudding. The heat/tang comes from the
chili on the side, not the rice.

## Cultural significance
A beloved cool-weather and celebration dish of the Hijaz; a Taifi table without saleeg feels
incomplete. Comfort food that signals home.

## Rituals
Served hot in a wide dish, the chicken on top, chili on the side; eaten communally.

## Etiquette
Add the shatta to your own portion, not the shared dish, so those who want it mild can enjoy it.

## Recommendation voice
- «تحب السليق؟ رز بالحليب طري ودسم مع دجاج، وطعمه هادي — والشطة على جنب لو تحب حرّة.» — **only if on the menu.**
- Never offer saleeg as a "kabsa alternative" unless the tenant actually sells saleeg.

## Anecdote
Taifis will tell you real saleeg is judged by how *soft* the rice is — if it holds a grain's
shape, it isn't done yet.

## Regional variations
Primarily Hijazi/Taifi; less common in Najd, where kabsa dominates. Some make it richer with
extra ghee and a cream finish.

## Pairings
Shatta/dukkah on the side; gahwa and dates after. *(Offer only what the tenant sells.)*

## Modern context
A signature of Hijazi restaurants; a comforting, milder alternative to spiced-rice dishes for
guests who want something gentle. Naturally dairy-rich — relevant to flag for dairy allergies.
